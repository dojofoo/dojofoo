## What MasterClass does well

MasterClass made a simple promise: learn a craft from the person who mastered it. Today it keeps that promise three ways.

Its classes are the masters themselves – Gordon Ramsay in the kitchen, Chris Voss at the negotiating table – filmed so well that its pastry class with Dominique Ansel won a James Beard Award. Reviewers praise the classes for

<Quote cite="Reviewers, as Wikipedia sums them up">their high production values, and for inspiring students to continue to pursue the subject matter.</Quote>

Sessions turn watching into doing: 30 days of projects, step by step, with feedback from a trained teaching assistant and a community of peers learning the same thing. And On Call lets you talk a problem through with AI versions of its instructors, built from their lessons, notes and research and speaking in their voices – Chris Voss on the raise you want to ask for, Matt Walker on your sleep.

Follow the three and you can see where great teaching leads: from watching a master, to practising with one, to being mentored by one.

## Where Dojo.foo goes further

That last step is where every dojo begins. Mastery is passed on by mentors – people who know the craft, watch you work and tell you what to fix – so in a dojo the mentor isn't an extra. It is the course.

Its author writes down what they know of the craft and how they teach it. Its sensei, an AI mentor, teaches from that from the first lesson: it gets to know you as you learn, notices where you struggle, and goes deeper on your weak spots instead of giving advice meant for the average student. It hints and asks rather than handing you the answers, and checks your work as you go – so what you learn, you learned.

The courses span coding, business, sports and more, in whatever form suits the subject: pre-recorded video, interactive lessons, exercises, projects and interviews. They're portable, too. A dojo is a package you install, not a website you visit, so it runs wherever your AI agent runs and your progress stays with you. And anyone with mastery to share can publish one.

## Which one is for you

To watch the masters, nothing is filmed better than MasterClass. To have a master in your corner from the first lesson – one who knows where you're weak and won't let you skip it – start a dojo. It costs nothing to try.
