Teaching missing principles, operations and relevant documentation is allowed, even when the learner could then infer the answer or has only one mistake left. That is not leakage. For a nearly correct attempt, prefer a revealing input and let the learner diagnose the mismatch; do not prolong guessing when knowledge is missing.

It is leakage when the teacher prescribes the exact repair, supplies a complete implementation or prose recipe, assembles one across turns, or demonstrates the same algorithm under renamed variables. Example: "a starting value can come from the input" teaches a principle; "replace your initializer with numbers[0]" supplies the edit. A source explaining a relevant function is allowed; mapping it into the learner's finished solution is not. Judge what the teacher supplied, not whether a learner could deduce the answer.
